1 Introduction and Raw Materials

Why Magnesium?

Essential for lightweight alloys in aerospace and automotive sectors. Extracting from seawater is more cost-effective than mining crust minerals.

Primary Source

Seawater! Mg²⁺ is the 2nd most abundant cation (after Na⁺), making up ~0.13% by weight.

Raw Materials

  • Bittern: Leftover solution from salt production (rich in Mg²⁺).
  • Disposable brine: From reverse-osmosis seawater.

1. Production of CaO

We need cheap OH⁻ ions to precipitate Mg²⁺. We get them by heating limestone/dolomite to make CaO.

$$ CaCO_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO(s) + CO_2(g) $$

$$ CaCO_3 \cdot MgCO_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO(s) + MgO(s) + 2CO_2(g) $$

Key Point

Incomplete heating leaves unreacted CaCO₃, contaminating the CaO.

2 & 3. Precipitation & Conversion

CaO reacts with water to form Ca(OH)₂, which pulls Mg²⁺ out as solid Mg(OH)₂. We then add HCl to convert it to a meltable chloride salt.

Precipitation

$$ Mg^{2+}(aq) + 2OH^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons Mg(OH)_2(s) \downarrow $$

Conversion

$$ Mg(OH)_2(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow MgCl_2(aq) + 2H_2O(l) $$

Risk

Ca(OH)₂ might co-precipitate, reducing purity.

Dolomite Bonus

Insoluble MgO from step 1 reacts directly with HCl here.

4. Drying & Electrolysis

Evaporate the water, melt the MgCl₂, and use electricity (700-800°C) to force Mg²⁺ to gain electrons and become pure metal.

Anode (+)

$$ 2Cl^-(l) \rightarrow Cl_2(g) + 2e^- $$

Cathode (-)

$$ Mg^{2+}(l) + 2e^- \rightarrow Mg(l) $$

Separation

Molten Mg floats on the denser MgCl₂ melt.

Recycling

Cl₂ gas is collected to regenerate HCl.

6 Electrochemical Cell Structure & Additional Details

Cell Structure

  • The cell consists of a steel container, a graphite anode, and a steel cathode with openings for molten Mg to travel through.
  • Fuel gases are supplied to maintain extreme heat required for the electrolysis process.

Industrial Applications

Magnesium alloys are highly valued in industries, especially in the aerospace and automobile sectors, due to their strength and lightness.

Environmental Disadvantages

A major drawback of the Dow Process is the emission of CO₂ during the decomposition of limestone/dolomite and the combustion of petroleum fuels required for high-temperature electrolysis.
