Rate of Reaction

2.1 Definition of Rate

The rate of a reaction is defined as the change in concentration of a substance with respect to time.

Symbolically, $$ \text{Rate} = \pm \frac{\Delta [C]}{\Delta t} $$

Where:

  • $[C]$ = Concentration
  • $t$ = Time
  • $\Delta$ = Change in quantity

The sign depends on the substance:

+ Positive Sign

Used for products because product concentration increases with time.

– Negative Sign

Used for reactants because reactant concentration decreases with time.

2.2 Overall Rate of Reaction

The overall rate of a reaction can be determined using the stoichiometric coefficients of the balanced equation.

Example Reaction:

$$ 2N_2O_5(g) \rightarrow 4NO_2(g) + O_2(g) $$

Rate of Reaction:

$$ \text{Rate} = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta [N_2O_5]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{4}\frac{\Delta [NO_2]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{1}\frac{\Delta [O_2]}{\Delta t} $$

This ensures that the rate is the same regardless of which substance is used.

Stoichiometry to Rate Relationship

2A Rate = -1/2 $\frac{\Delta[A]}{\Delta t}$
→
3B Rate = +1/3 $\frac{\Delta[B]}{\Delta t}$

2.3 Concentration–Time Graph

A concentration vs time graph can be used to determine different types of reaction rates.

Three types of rates can be obtained from this graph:

1. Initial Rate

The rate at the beginning of the reaction ($t=0$). Found by drawing a tangent at the start of the curve.

2. Instantaneous Rate

The rate at a specific point in time. Found by drawing a tangent at that point on the curve.

3. Average Rate

Average rate is given by: $$ \text{Avg Rate} = \frac{\text{Total Change in Concentration}}{\text{Total Time}} $$
